Discrete Structures

Bulacan State University
Bustos Campus

Hi, I am RJ Nieto, and I will be your instructor for this module. I graduated with a BSc in Mathematics with first-class honours from the University of Bristol in the United Kingdom. I am currently pursuing an MSc in Applied and Computational Mathematics at The Johns Hopkins University in the United States.

You can simply call me RJ, Kuya RJ, or Sir—though “Sir” sounds a little icky because I have not been knighted by King Charles. Please spare me Tito RJ or Daddy RJ, though.

I will use this page to share course information, lecture notes, examples, exercises, and supplementary material for our Discrete Structures module at Bulacan State University’s Bustos Campus. As first-year BS Information Technology students, you will use these materials throughout the semester.

To be frank, this course is discrete mathematics. Whatever the title may suggest to you, you should expect a demanding first-year mathematics course. I expect everyone to engage deeply with the material. The keys to mastering mathematics are:

  1. Practice, constant practice. Work through problems yourself and revisit the ones you find difficult.
  2. Curiosity. Ask questions, even when they sound silly to you.
  3. Diligence. Keep up with the material and work through your mistakes.

We will not be solving Olympiad-level problems, and you do not need exceptional mathematical aptitude to do well here. Think of this module as functional mathematical literacy for future IT specialists. We will work with logic, sets, functions, relations, counting, integers, induction, recursion, graphs, and trees, connecting these ideas to organising records, expressing access rules, and modelling networks.

If you want to learn how to wrangle information, mathematics will give you the tools to do it precisely and systematically.

BulSU aims to be a globally competitive university, and I want you to take pride in your studies. Drawing on my own experience, particularly in the United Kingdom, I will teach you with the same expectations of independent study, careful reasoning, and active participation that I encountered at a leading university abroad.

Course policies

These class arrangements should be read alongside the BulSU Undergraduate Student Manual (Part II, Sections F–G and J–L) and the Enhanced Guidelines on Student Conduct, Discipline, and Grievance Intervention, which BulSU identifies as approved under BOR Resolution No. 34, series of 2022. The approved syllabus and applicable university policies take precedence over these class arrangements.

  1. Electronic devices: Phones and other personal electronic devices must be switched off and put away during class. Approved accessibility arrangements and university-required exceptions take precedence.
  2. Photo: Please bring a recent 1×1 photo to our first class. This will help me identify students when recording attendance.
  3. Seating: We will use a fixed seating plan to facilitate attendance-taking and the recording of class recitation.
  4. Attention and classroom conduct: We meet only once a week for three hours, so each session can be more cognitively demanding than a shorter class that meets twice a week. I expect everyone to listen intently and stay focused, even when the material gets difficult. If you are struggling to follow, you are strongly encouraged to interrupt me and ask questions, or raise them during consultation hours. Disruptive chatting, excessive noise, and other misbehaviour are unacceptable and will be addressed through the university’s prescribed disciplinary procedures. Participation marks follow the approved assessment criteria. I am training you for real life, and success in real life requires discipline.
  5. Coursework and deadlines: Submit coursework by the stated deadline. Work not submitted by the deadline receives zero marks, unless an excused absence or another university-approved arrangement applies. An unexcused absence does not extend a deadline or entitle you to a make-up assessment. Work submitted on time is assessed under the published criteria.
  6. Attendance and punctuality: Attend promptly and regularly. The handbook marks arrival 15 minutes after the scheduled start as tardy and 20 minutes after the start as absent; being marked absent does not prevent you from attending the lesson. Unexcused absences exceeding 20% of required class time before midterms trigger automatic dropping, recorded as FDA (failure due to absences) or UD (unofficially dropped). Against the current 42-hour teaching timetable, a third full-session absence totals nine hours and exceeds 20%; keep unexcused absences to no more than two sessions. The official calculation follows the required semester contact hours, and the count does not reset after midterms. Attendance classifications and grades follow university rules; FDA/UD and a numerical grade of 5.0 are distinct classifications.
  7. Missed examinations: If you miss an examination for an excused reason, contact me promptly with supporting documentation. Special examinations require the instructor’s recommendation and the unit head’s approval under Part II, Section J. Your final examination is not automatically double-weighted. An unexcused missed examination receives zero marks under the course assessment arrangements, subject to applicable university policies.
  8. Excused absences: The handbook recognises official university representation, medically certified sickness, and force majeure as excused reasons. Notify me promptly and provide the relevant documentation. An excused absence does not waive coursework requirements; arrange to complete missed work. The handbook also sets a 30% limit on excused absences.
  9. Assessment and concerns: Assessment components, weights, and marking criteria follow the approved syllabus. You may ask how your marks were calculated and request a review of your class standing. Raise concerns with me or use the university’s student grievance procedure.
  10. Borderline marks: I may extend consideration to students with borderline failing marks who have perfect attendance, where permitted by the approved assessment rules. This is discretionary and does not guarantee a passing grade or waive academic requirements.

Course-specific arrangements and updates will be communicated through official class channels.
